# The Indus Valley Civilisation (Harappan Civilisation) Knowledge Base
**Era:** Bronze Age Urban Dawn (Mature Harappan Period, c. 2600 BCE – 1900 BCE)  
**Geographical Scope:** Indus-Saraswati Basin, Gujarat, Sindh, Punjab, Haryana, Balochistan, Western Uttar Pradesh  
**Key Excavation Sites:** Harappa (Punjab, 1921 - Daya Ram Sahni), Mohenjo-daro (Sindh, 1922 - R.D. Banerji), Dholavira (Kutch, Gujarat - R.S. Bisht), Lothal (Gujarat - S.R. Rao), Kalibangan (Rajasthan - A. Ghosh), Rakhigarhi (Haryana - Amarendra Nath)

---

## 1. Architectural & Urban Engineering

### 1.1 City Planning & The Grid System
- **Orientation & Gridiron Layout:** Harappan cities were meticulously planned on a cardinal grid system with north-south and east-west thoroughfares crossing at right angles.
- **Twin-Division Settlement Structure:**
  - **The Western Citadel (Acropolis):** Raised on monumental mud-brick platforms (up to 7 meters high at Mohenjo-daro) to protect against recurrent seasonal inundations. Housed public administrative buildings, granaries, assembly halls, and the Great Bath.
  - **The Eastern Lower Town:** The residential and artisan quarters laid out in blocks (insulae), where houses opened onto quiet side alleys rather than noisy main thoroughfares, maintaining privacy and dust protection.
- **Standardized Baked Brick Metric (1:2:4 Ratio):**
  - Unlike contemporary Mesopotamia and Egypt which primarily used sun-dried mud bricks, Harappans used uniform kiln-fired bricks with a universal dimensional ratio of **1 (thickness) : 2 (width) : 4 (length)** (typically 7 × 14 × 28 cm).
  - Bricks were joined using alternating headers and stretchers—a technique now globally known as "English Bond", invented 4,000 years prior by Harappan masons.

### 1.2 The Great Bath of Mohenjo-daro
- **Dimensions & Construction:** Located in the northern sector of the Citadel, measuring approximately 11.88 meters (north-south) by 7.01 meters (east-west), with a depth of 2.43 meters.
- **Hydraulic Waterproofing:** The floor and walls were built of finely fitted baked bricks laid on edge with gypsum mortar, backed by a 2.5-centimeter-thick layer of natural bitumen (asphalt)—the world's earliest known municipal waterproofing engineering.
- **Water Supply & Drainage:** Supplied with water from a dedicated corbelled-brick well in an adjacent room; emptied through an enormous corbelled arched culvert tall enough for a person to walk through for regular desiltation and maintenance.
- **Sacral Purpose:** Flanked by eight private dressing cubicles and a double flight of brick steps with wooden treads; universally regarded as a sacred site for ritual purification prior to entering the assembly halls.

### 1.3 World's First Municipal Drainage & Sanitary System
- **House-to-Street Drainage:** Every residential home featured a private bathing area with sloping paved floors connected via terracotta drainage spouts through exterior walls into covered street drains.
- **Inspection Manholes & Sump Pits:** Main streets featured deep masonry drainage channels covered with dressed limestone or terracotta slabs. Regular sump pits and sediment traps prevented solid debris from clogging the subterranean flow toward outside wastewater zones.

### 1.4 Dholavira's Giant Water Reservoirs & Signboard
- **Water Engineering in an Arid Zone:** Located on Khadir Bet in the Rann of Kutch, Dholavira engineered 16 massive rock-cut and masonry reservoirs that stored over 300,000 cubic meters of seasonal rainwater channeled from the ephemeral Manhar and Mandsar streams.
- **The World's Earliest Signboard:** Discovered in the northern gate of the citadel—ten large gypsum Indus script symbols (each approximately 37 cm high) mounted on a wooden board.

### 1.5 The World's First Tidal Dockyard at Lothal
- **Maritime Commerce Hub:** Located in the Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay), Gujarat. Features an engineered brick basin measuring 214 × 36 meters with an operational sluice gate and spillway designed to receive ocean vessels at high tide.
- Provided direct maritime trade connection to ancient **Meluhha** (the Mesopotamian Akkadian term for the Indus civilisation), Dilmun (Bahrain), and Magan (Oman).

## 2. Material Culture, Glyptic Art & Script

### 2.1 Steatite Seals & The Undeciphered Indus Script
- **Material & Craftsmanship:** Thousands of square and rectangular seals carved from soft talc/steatite stone, coated with an alkali slip and fired in kilns to produce a durable white luster.
- **Iconography:**
  - **The Unicorn Seal (Ekasringa):** The single most ubiquitous motif (~60% of all zoomorphic seals), depicting a mythical one-horned bovine standing before an enigmatic incense burner or ritual manger.
  - **The Pashupati Seal (Proto-Shiva):** Discovered at Mohenjo-daro by Sir John Marshall. Features a horned, three-faced yogic figure in padmasana posture, surrounded by an elephant, tiger, rhinoceros, water buffalo, and two horned deer at his feet.
  - **The Zebu / Humped Bull:** Celebrated for majestic muscular realism with a dewlap and prominent hump.
- **Indus Script:** A logo-syllabic script consisting of over 400 distinct signs written typically from **right to left**, as evidenced by cramping on the left margin. Used primarily for merchant branding, ownership stamps on clay tags (sealings), and administrative verification.

### 2.2 Metallurgy & The "Lost Wax" (Cire Perdue) Technique
- **The Bronze Dancing Girl of Mohenjo-daro:**
  - Discovered in 1926 by Ernest Mackay in the HR area of Mohenjo-daro.
  - Cast in bronze using the sophisticated lost-wax technique (*cire perdue*).
  - Stands 10.5 cm high in a confident *tribhanga* pose, resting her right hand on her hip while her left arm is adorned from wrist to shoulder with 24-25 bangles, holding a small bowl.
- **The Priest-King Bust:**
  - Carved from white steatite (soapstone), 17.5 cm high.
  - Depicts a bearded dignitary with combed hair held by a fillet headband, wearing an embroidered trefoil-patterned shawl draped over the left shoulder, leaving the right arm bare.

### 2.3 Bead Manufacturing & Standardized Metrology
- **Etched Carnelian Beads:** Lothal and Chanhudaro housed specialized bead workshops. Semi-precious stones (carnelian, lapis lazuli, turquoise, jasper) were drilled with micro-diamond drills and etched with white alkaline designs using heating techniques.
- **Chert Cube Weights:** Strict uniform binary and decimal weight system (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64... up to decimal divisions) made from polished chert cubes, standardized across a territory exceeding 1 million square kilometers without significant regional deviation.

## 3. Social Order & Economy

- **Egalitarian Governance vs. Autocracy:** Unlike Mesopotamia and Egypt with grandiose imperial palaces, royal tombs, and depictions of conquering warrior kings, Harappan society displays remarkable civic equality, lack of standing armies or weapons of conquest, and decentralized civic governance.
- **Agricultural Base:** Cultivation of barley, wheat, sessamum, mustard, and the world's earliest evidence of cotton cultivation (which the Greeks called *Sindon* after the Sindhu river).
- **Decline (c. 1900 BCE):** Not sudden invasion, but progressive ecological shift: tectonic shifts deflecting the Saraswati river course, prolonged monsoon weakening, shifting trade routes, and soil desalination leading to peaceful regional rural dispersal rather than catastrophic annihilation.
